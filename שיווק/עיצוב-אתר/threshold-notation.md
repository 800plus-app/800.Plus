# Threshold Notation

*A design philosophy*

---

## The Movement

Threshold Notation concerns itself with the interval — the measurable pause between encounter and comprehension, between the eye landing on a form and the mind claiming it. This movement holds that the most consequential human experiences occur in intervals too brief to narrate and too significant to ignore, and that such intervals can be *notated*: recorded, gridded, accumulated, and studied with the patience of a field scientist cataloguing specimens across a lifetime. The work does not illustrate the threshold. It *documents* it, with the sobriety of an instrument and the restraint of someone who has spent years learning what to leave out.

## Space and Form

The compositions are built from **dense accumulation of minimal marks** — the single stroke, repeated thousands of times, each one nearly identical to its neighbor and each one subtly distinct. Meaning arises not from any individual mark but from the *field* they generate together: a population, a distribution, a weather system of small decisions. Vast margins surround these fields, and this negative space is not empty but held — the way a laboratory bench is cleared before the work begins. The eye should first perceive an overall tone, then, on approach, discover that the tone is made of countable things. This shift from field to particle is the central experience, and achieving it demands **painstaking calibration** of scale, spacing, and density across the entire surface.

## Color and Material

The palette is severely limited — a warm ground of aged paper or bone, a single ink that reads almost black, and one restrained accent that appears rarely enough to feel like a finding rather than a decoration. Color is never used to please; it is used to **encode**. Where a mark is denser, darker, more present, that is information. Where it fades toward the ground, that too is information. The surface carries a faint grain, the memory of pressed fibre, so that the eye reads material rather than screen. Every chromatic decision must feel arrived at rather than chosen, the residue of **countless discarded alternatives** by someone at the absolute top of their craft.

## Scale and Rhythm

Rhythm is the movement's true instrument. Regular intervals establish a baseline pulse; deliberate breaks in that pulse carry all the emotional weight. A field of evenly spaced strokes is a held breath; a widened gap is a caught one. Scale operates across two extremes and nothing between — the monumental single gesture and the near-microscopic notation — with the vast middle register left deliberately vacant. This refusal of the comfortable middle is what separates **master-level execution** from competence, and it must be defended at every stage of the work.

## Composition and Hierarchy

The page is organized as a scientific plate from a discipline that does not exist: axis marks along an edge, sequential reference numerals, a legend that explains almost nothing, a specimen presented at centre with unwarranted reverence. Typography is **clinical and thin**, set small, functioning as apparatus rather than voice — save for a single anchor, one word or one number, permitted to carry weight because it has earned it. Nothing overlaps. Nothing crowds the edge. Every element sits within generous margins, and the alignments must survive close inspection with a straightedge, because **this work will be looked at closely and it must reward that looking**.

## The Standard

Threshold Notation is uncompromising about finish. The result must appear as the product of **many hundreds of hours** — a plate engraved rather than printed, a diagram redrawn until the hand disappeared. Any suggestion of speed, of a default, of an untested decision, breaks the entire proposition. The viewer must sense that someone counted, and then counted again. The paradox at the movement's heart is this: it applies the cold apparatus of measurement to something that cannot be measured, and in doing so it argues that the ephemeral deserves the same **meticulous, expert, unhurried attention** we reserve for the observable world.
