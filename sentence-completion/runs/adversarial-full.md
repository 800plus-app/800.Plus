## Corpus-level notes

1. **Dominant defect: synonym-cluster options with a stem that constrains meaning but not collocation.** Roughly two-thirds of the FAILs are items where the writer built four near-synonyms and then wrote a stem specific enough to pick a *sense* but not specific enough to pick a *word* (9, 18, 45, 49, 52, 53, 86, 100, 120, 128, 141, 159, 169, 189, 202). Item 179 is the extreme case: two options differ only in "amass" vs "accumulate".

2. **A second cluster is the opposite failure — three dead distractors.** In 5, 8, 24, 32, 36, 38, 65, 77, 90, 101, 105, 113, 115, 116, 130, 136, 157, 192, 204 the distractors are not words a competent writer would consider, and a single adjective, preposition or number in the stem fixes the answer. These are the items that would produce the 100%-with-options / 15%-without-options gap.

3. **Recurring surface tells of translation:** "the hour" for appointment time (7, 161); "its first colours" for "original colours" (143); "one skill" for "a skill" (87); "lost all awareness of the hours passing" for "lost all sense of time" (174).

4. **Connectors are used as decoration, not as logic.** "moreover" / "furthermore" / "likewise" repeatedly join clauses that stand in no additive or parallel relation (1, 23, 74, 98), and in 28 and 95 the causal connective actually contradicts the sentence it introduces.

5. **Two items have no defensible answer at all** — 185 (all four options singular, stem demands a plural) and 193 (context demands "brittle"/"fissile", which is not offered, and calls slate "fragile").
